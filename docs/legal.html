In short: NetherFlow is a free, open-source music player made by Volodya.rua (GNU GPL v3). It doesn't host any music, videos or lyrics: it finds them on other services (YouTube and YouTube Music, Audius, Apple's iTunes catalog, LRCLIB, NetEase Cloud Music and Kugou) and shows them to you directly from those services. If you choose to save a song for offline listening, the app downloads it from YouTube and keeps it inside the app, on your device only. NetherFlow has no accounts, no ads of its own and no analytics, and the developer receives none of your data.

2. What NetherFlow does

It also plays podcasts, from each show's own feed and host, and audiobooks recorded by LibriVox's volunteers, from the Internet Archive. LibriVox's recordings are in the public domain in the United States; some of the books may still be under copyright in other countries (where copyright lasts longer after an author's death), so check the law where you live before you listen to or save one. 6. Saving for offline listening, and story clips

NetherFlow can save a song you choose, with its cover and, if you choose, its lyrics, so you can listen without an internet connection. To do this it downloads the song's audio from its YouTube upload with yt-dlp, an open-source download tool included with the app, just as YouTube serves it (nothing is re-recorded or re-encoded); or, when the artist's own upload of the song is on Audius, the file Audius serves for it. Saved songs stay inside the app on your device, for your own personal listening only: you must not extract, copy, share, sell or distribute them.

YouTube's Terms of Service don't allow downloading content unless YouTube itself shows a download button for it, so saving songs this way breaks YouTube's terms. It may also not be permitted by the rights holders or by copyright law where you live. Whether to use it is your decision and your responsibility. Only save content you are allowed to keep a copy of, and delete saved songs if you are asked to or are no longer allowed to keep them. The developer provides no content and never receives the songs you save.

A podcast episode or an audiobook's chapter is saved as the file its host publishes for listeners to download (no yt-dlp): the podcast's own host, or the Internet Archive for LibriVox's recordings, which are in the public domain in the United States (see section 2). Saved episodes and chapters stay inside the app on your device too; a podcast stays its publisher's, under its own terms.

"Make a story" creates a short video (about 15 seconds of the song, with its cover and lyrics) for you to share. Whether to post it is your decision and your responsibility: the song, cover and lyrics belong to their owners, and the platform you post to (for example Instagram) applies its own music and copyright rules and may mute or remove it.

Privacy Policy

Summary

NetherFlow has no accounts, no analytics, no tracking and no ads of its own, and since its source code is public, anyone can check that. The developer does not collect, receive, store or sell any personal data. To work, the app talks directly from your device to the third-party services listed below, which receive the information described there under their own privacy policies.

Stored only on your device

  • Your settings: mood, background and its quality (or the quality the app picked for your device), lyric animation (and Extreme lyrics, their look, emoji and music video), font, size and alignment, timeline and beat styles, the island and its glow, volume, repeat, equalizer and loudness.
  • Lyric-sync adjustments you made for individual songs.
  • Your search history: recent searches and recently played songs (you can clear it in the search screen).
  • Your library: liked songs and playlists (and playlists you import).
  • Your listening stats: time listened by day, hour, song, artist and genre (Library › Your stats).
  • Autoplay's memory: the songs you've heard lately (so they aren't picked again soon) and how much you like each artist it picks, from what you skip, play through or like.
  • Songs, podcast episodes and audiobook chapters you saved for offline listening, with their covers and, if you chose, their lyrics (you can remove them in the Library).
  • Podcasts you follow, audiobooks you're listening to, where you left each episode and chapter, and your listening speed for them.
  • The headphones you picked for headphone correction (Equalizer › Headphones), and its settings.
  • That you accepted these terms, and which version.
  • Phone ↔ PC sync copies your library, history and stats directly between your own devices on your local network, encrypted with a key made from the code shown: it never goes through anyone else's server. Listen together sends only what the session needs (the song, where it's at, names, reactions), encrypted, through a public relay.
  • Sharing a playlist (Library › Share) makes a link and a QR code that carry the playlist's name and its songs. Nothing is uploaded: the playlist is inside the link itself, and anyone you give it to can see what's in it. Opening the link shows a page on GitHub Pages (github.io), which, like any website, receives the opener's IP address; the part of the link holding the songs is not sent to it. That page looks the songs up at Apple and shows their covers from Apple and YouTube.
  • A playlist you edit together with others ("Edit together") is the exception: so that everyone with its link can open and change it, it is kept on the public relays described below, encrypted on your device with a key made from the code in its link. Anyone who has the link can see and change the playlist, so share it only with people you trust; "Stop editing together" keeps your own copy and stops sending changes; the copy on the relays stays for the others who have the link, until the relays drop it. Opening such a link on its web page reads it from those relays too.

This stays on your device and is never sent to the developer. Uninstalling the app (or clearing its data) deletes it.
